Supplements

6 options, ranked by how strong the research is for IBS.

Peppermint oil (enteric-coated)
Good evidence
Relaxes smooth muscle in the gut wall, reducing spasms and abdominal pain. A 2014 meta-analysis of 9 RCTs found peppermint oil significantly more effective than placebo for global IBS symptoms.

Probiotics
Mixed evidence
Specific strains (notably Bifidobacterium infantis 35624) show meaningful effects on bloating and pain in some trials. Results are highly variable — strain specificity matters enormously.
Aloe vera (decolourised)
Limited evidence
Two small trials suggest symptom reduction in IBS, particularly for stool frequency. Evidence is limited by study size, but well-tolerated with a low side-effect profile.
STW 5 (Iberogast)
Moderate evidence
A herbal blend of nine plant extracts including peppermint, chamomile, and caraway. Several RCTs show significant improvement in abdominal pain and overall IBS symptoms. Well-tolerated and widely available.
Glutamine
Moderate evidence
An amino acid that supports gut barrier integrity. Particularly promising for IBS-D following a gut infection (post-infectious IBS) — one RCT showed significant reduction in bowel frequency and permeability.
Artichoke leaf extract
Limited evidence
Traditionally used as a digestive aid. A small number of trials show improvement in bloating and overall IBS symptoms — not yet supported by large-scale evidence.
