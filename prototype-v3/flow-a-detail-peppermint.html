Peppermint oil (enteric-coated)

A well-tolerated supplement that relaxes smooth muscle in the gut wall. A 2014 meta-analysis found peppermint oil was significantly more effective than placebo for reducing global IBS symptoms and abdominal pain.

What it is

Peppermint oil's active ingredient, menthol, works by blocking calcium channels in the smooth muscle lining the gut wall. This reduces spasms and cramping — the mechanism behind abdominal pain in many IBS presentations. Enteric coating is essential: it prevents the oil releasing in the stomach (which causes heartburn) and ensures it reaches the small intestine and colon where it does its work.

How research suggests using it
1
Choose the right product
Look specifically for enteric-coated peppermint oil capsules — not peppermint tea, peppermint extract, or standard (non-coated) capsules. Brands labelled "IBS peppermint oil" are usually enteric-coated.
2
Take before meals
The dose used in most studies is one capsule, three times daily, taken 30–60 minutes before meals. Taking with food can interfere with the coating's timing.
3
Allow 4 weeks before judging
Most responders see a difference within 2–4 weeks. Studies use 4 weeks as the minimum trial period before assessing whether it's working.
Where this evidence comes from
Good evidence
A 2014 meta-analysis of 9 RCTs found peppermint oil was significantly more effective than placebo for both global symptom relief and abdominal pain specifically, with a low rate of serious side effects.
What this means — the evidence base is solid (9 independent RCTs all pointing the same direction), but some studies were small and short. That's why this is graded Good rather than Strong. It's a meaningful effect with a good safety profile — the uncertainty is mainly about long-term use beyond 4 weeks.

Worth knowing

The evidence is strongest for abdominal pain and cramping — if bloating or diarrhoea are your primary symptoms, peppermint oil may be less effective as a standalone intervention. It's also not recommended if you have gastro-oesophageal reflux disease (GORD/GERD): even enteric-coated capsules can occasionally cause heartburn in people with reflux. Mention it to your GP if you're on any regular medication, as there are a small number of known interactions.
