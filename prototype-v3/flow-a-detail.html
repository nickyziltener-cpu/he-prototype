Low FODMAP diet

A short-term elimination diet that removes fermentable carbohydrates linked to IBS symptoms. In the largest meta-analysis of 7 RCTs, 57% of participants saw meaningful symptom relief — vs 26% on a control diet.

What it is

FODMAPs are a group of carbs that ferment in the gut and can trigger bloating, pain and irregular bowel movements. The diet removes them for a few weeks, then reintroduces them one at a time so you can find your personal triggers.

How research suggests using it
1
Strict elimination
Remove all high-FODMAP foods for 4–8 weeks. Common culprits: onions, garlic, beans, wheat, lactose-heavy dairy.
2
Reintroduce one at a time
Add food groups back individually over 3 days each to find what your gut tolerates.
3
Personalise long-term
Most people settle at a personal tolerance — not a forever-restricted plate.
Where this evidence comes from
Strong evidence
A 2022 meta-analysis of 7 RCTs (n=1,242) found that 57% of participants saw meaningful symptom relief, vs 26% on a control diet — consistent across all included trials.
What this means — a meta-analysis pools and re-analyses multiple independent trials. Consistent effects across 7 separate trial populations is much harder to explain by chance. That's why this is graded Strong.

Examples of high-FODMAP foods to avoid during elimination
Vegetables
Onions · Garlic · Shallots · Leek · Asparagus · Artichoke · Cauliflower · Mushrooms · Celery (large amounts)
Fruit
Apples · Pears · Mangoes · Cherries · Peaches · Nectarines · Watermelon · Dried fruit
Grains & bread
Wheat · Rye · Barley — including most bread, pasta, couscous, and crackers made from them
Dairy
Cow's milk · Soft cheeses (ricotta, cottage) · Yoghurt · Custard · Ice cream
Legumes
Kidney beans · Lentils · Chickpeas · Baked beans · Black beans
Sweeteners
Honey · Agave · High-fructose corn syrup · Sorbitol · Mannitol · Xylitol (common in sugar-free products)
This is a selection of common examples — not a complete list. The full FODMAP database (500+ foods with portion thresholds) is maintained by Monash University. A registered dietitian can give you a full reference sheet.
Worth knowing

The elimination phase is a short diagnostic tool — not a permanent way of eating. Broad long-term restriction can affect gut microbiome diversity. The reintroduction phase is how you find your actual triggers. Once you know what's causing symptoms, permanently avoiding just those foods is completely fine — and that's the goal.
