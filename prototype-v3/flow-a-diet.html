Diet

6 options, ranked by how strong the research is for IBS-C.

Low FODMAP diet
Strong evidence
An 8-week elimination-and-reintroduction trial. The most studied dietary change for IBS — 50–80% see meaningful symptom relief.
Learn more
Mediterranean diet
Good evidence
An anti-inflammatory eating pattern linked to lower symptom severity and better gut diversity. Especially relevant for IBS-C.
Soluble fibre (psyllium)
Good evidence
Gradually adding soluble fibre eases constipation-dominant symptoms. Works for most subtypes — the unsexy one that actually helps.
Gluten restriction
Moderate evidence
Some people with IBS-D see improvement — though it's unclear whether gluten itself or the FODMAPs in wheat are the real driver. A useful trial if Low FODMAP alone doesn't resolve symptoms.
Low-fat diet
Moderate evidence
Reducing dietary fat can ease post-meal pain and urgency — fat is a potent trigger of gut contractions. Modest but consistent effect seen in trials, particularly for IBS-D.
Specific carbohydrate diet (SCD)
Limited evidence
A more restrictive approach that eliminates most complex carbohydrates. Small studies show benefit but no large RCTs yet. Better evidence exists for related dietary approaches.
