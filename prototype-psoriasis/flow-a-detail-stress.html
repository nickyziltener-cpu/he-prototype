Mind & Body Good evidence

Stress management (MBSR)

Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction was among the first psychological interventions to show measurable skin outcome improvements in psoriasis. Kabat-Zinn's landmark study found that patients who practised mindfulness during phototherapy cleared significantly faster than controls receiving only phototherapy.

Why stress matters in psoriasis

Psoriasis is deeply entangled with the stress response. Stress triggers the release of neuropeptides that activate skin immune cells (mast cells, dendritic cells), which in turn amplify the T-cell-driven inflammation responsible for plaques. The result is a bidirectional cycle: stress worsens psoriasis, and psoriasis — being a visible, stigmatised condition — generates further psychological stress. MBSR intervenes at the root of this cycle.

How research suggests using it
1
Start with 10 minutes daily
Daily practice is more effective than longer but infrequent sessions. Even 10 minutes of body-scan or breath-focused meditation builds the neural pathways that reduce reactivity over time.
2
Consider a structured 8-week MBSR programme
The original Kabat-Zinn protocol is an 8-week course — weekly 2.5-hour sessions plus daily home practice. Many NHS trusts offer MBSR via referral; online versions are also available.
3
Practice during treatment sessions
Kabat-Zinn's original study used an audio-guided body scan during phototherapy. If you're receiving UVB, practising mindfulness during the session may accelerate benefit — the combination effect was the study's key finding.
4
Track flares alongside stress patterns
Journalling stress levels alongside flare patterns gives you concrete data on your personal stress-skin relationship — and reinforces motivation to maintain the practice.
Where this evidence comes from
Good evidence
Jon Kabat-Zinn's 1998 study (n=37) randomly assigned patients to phototherapy alone vs. phototherapy plus mindfulness audio guidance. The mindfulness group reached clinical clearing 4x faster. Subsequent systematic reviews have confirmed the stress-skin relationship and psychological intervention effects, though large RCTs remain limited.
What this means — the Kabat-Zinn study is small by modern standards. The evidence grade is Good, not Strong, because the body of RCT evidence is still developing. But the plausibility of the mechanism is very high, the safety profile is excellent, and the quality-of-life benefits are consistent across studies regardless of skin outcome.

For psoriasis specifically, the body scan practice is the most studied format — it involves systematically moving attention through body sensations without judgment.
This isn't "just relaxation"

Mindfulness gets dismissed as a soft intervention, but its mechanisms are neurobiological. Consistent practice measurably reduces cortisol and pro-inflammatory cytokines. For a condition driven by immune dysregulation, that's clinically meaningful — not just "feeling calmer". It also doesn't interfere with any other treatment you're using.
