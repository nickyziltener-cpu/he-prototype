Diet & nutrition

4 options — the strongest evidence is for lifestyle changes and anti-inflammatory patterns.

Mediterranean-style diet
Good evidence
The most studied dietary approach for psoriasis. The 2025 MEDIPSO randomised trial found 47% of participants achieved 75% skin improvement vs 0% in controls. Barrea et al. (2019) established the observational foundation.
Learn more
Weight management
Good evidence
Obesity is an independent risk factor for psoriasis severity. Studies consistently show that weight reduction — even modest — correlates with improved skin outcomes and better treatment response.
Alcohol reduction
Good evidence
Alcohol is a well-established psoriasis trigger. Cohort studies show that heavy drinking correlates with worse severity and reduced treatment response — cutting back is one of the highest-yield lifestyle changes.
Gluten-free diet (if coeliac or NCGS)
Moderate evidence
Psoriasis has elevated rates of coeliac disease co-occurrence. For those with confirmed coeliac or non-coeliac gluten sensitivity, going gluten-free may meaningfully reduce severity. Not clearly beneficial without confirmed sensitivity.
Low-calorie / anti-inflammatory eating
Moderate evidence
Reducing inflammatory foods (ultra-processed foods, red meat, refined sugar) and increasing vegetables, legumes, and whole grains shows broad benefit in inflammatory skin conditions.
