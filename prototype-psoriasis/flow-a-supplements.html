Supplements

4 options — honest about where the evidence is strong and where it isn't.

Omega-3 fatty acids (fish oil)
Moderate evidence
Strong anti-inflammatory mechanism via EPA and DHA — but a 2019 meta-analysis of 13 controlled trials found no significant PASI improvement overall. Evidence is more consistent for joint symptoms in psoriatic arthritis than for skin. Worth considering if you have joint involvement.
Vitamin D supplementation
Good evidence
Psoriasis patients frequently have low vitamin D levels, and supplementation has an immune-modulating role. Note: topical vitamin D analogues (calcipotriol) have stronger evidence — this is for oral supplementation.
Turmeric / curcumin
Moderate evidence
Curcumin has demonstrated anti-inflammatory properties in small RCTs for psoriasis. The challenge is bioavailability — formulations with piperine (black pepper extract) improve absorption significantly.
Zinc supplementation
Limited evidence
Zinc deficiency is more common in psoriasis patients — but the only dedicated plaque psoriasis RCT (n=25) found no significant benefit. Some evidence for joint symptoms in psoriatic arthritis. Association with deficiency doesn't mean supplementation helps if your levels are normal.
Probiotics
Limited evidence
Emerging research links gut microbiome changes to psoriasis. A few small trials show modest benefit with specific strains. Promising direction but not yet consistently replicated. Watch this space.
