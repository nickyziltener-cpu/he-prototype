Mind & body

5 options — particularly relevant if stress or smoking is a factor for you.

Stress management (MBSR)
Good evidence
Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction. Kabat-Zinn's landmark study showed faster plaque clearing in patients who combined MBSR with phototherapy. Addresses the stress-itch-flare cycle directly.
Learn more
Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT)
Good evidence
Addresses the psychological burden of visible skin conditions — shame, avoidance, body image. Multiple studies show CBT reduces psychological distress and secondarily improves physical symptoms through better self-management.
Regular exercise
Good evidence
Exercise reduces systemic inflammation and addresses comorbid metabolic risk — psoriasis significantly elevates cardiovascular risk, making regular activity particularly valuable. The best evidence comes from combined diet-and-exercise trials (Naldi et al., 2014, n=303). Also improves biologic treatment response.
Smoking cessation
Good evidence
Smoking consistently predicts worse psoriasis severity and reduces how well treatments work — particularly biologics and phototherapy. Multiple large cohort studies confirm the association. Quitting is one of the changes with the broadest effect across skin, cardiovascular risk, and treatment response.
Yoga & relaxation practices
Moderate evidence
Small controlled studies show measurable quality-of-life improvements. Yoga combines the anti-inflammatory effects of exercise with stress reduction. Effect on skin lesions is more modest than on psychological wellbeing.
Balneotherapy (Dead Sea / salt baths)
Moderate evidence
Dead Sea salt bathing has consistent observational evidence for plaque psoriasis. Proposed mechanism is a combination of UV exposure, mineral content, and stress reduction. Limited by poor RCT quality.
