Diet Good evidence

Mediterranean-style diet

The most studied dietary approach for psoriasis. A 2025 randomised trial (MEDIPSO) found that 47% of participants following a Mediterranean diet achieved 75% reduction in psoriasis severity — compared to 0% in the control group. Observational evidence has consistently pointed in the same direction.

Why diet matters in psoriasis

Psoriasis is fundamentally an inflammatory condition — and diet is one of the most modifiable factors influencing the body's inflammatory state. The Mediterranean diet reduces levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF-α, CRP), which are the same signalling molecules that drive psoriatic plaques. Obesity, poor metabolic health, and high ultra-processed food intake all amplify psoriasis severity — the Mediterranean pattern addresses all three.

What a Mediterranean-style diet looks like
Eat more of
Vegetables (especially leafy greens) · Legumes (lentils, chickpeas, beans) · Whole grains · Oily fish (salmon, mackerel, sardines — 2–3×/week) · Extra virgin olive oil · Nuts and seeds · Fresh fruit · Herbs and spices
Eat less of
Red and processed meat · Ultra-processed foods · Refined carbohydrates and added sugar · Butter and saturated fats · Alcohol (especially spirits)
Main anti-inflammatory mechanism
High polyphenol content (from vegetables, olive oil, and fruit) · Omega-3 to omega-6 ratio improvement · Improved insulin sensitivity and metabolic health
This isn't a strict diet with forbidden foods. It's an eating pattern — think of it as proportions and habits rather than rules.
Where this evidence comes from
Good evidence
The MEDIPSO randomised trial (2025) assigned 19 participants to a Mediterranean diet intervention and found 47.4% achieved PASI75 (75% skin improvement) at 16 weeks versus 0% in controls. This is the first RCT for this intervention in psoriasis. The earlier Barrea et al. (2019) case-control study (n=62) showed consistent observational associations, and subsequent systematic reviews have confirmed the pattern across multiple studies.
What this means — upgraded to Good evidence following the 2025 MEDIPSO trial. The trial is small (n=19), so this is a confident direction rather than settled science. The observational foundation has been solid for years. The diet has documented cardiovascular and metabolic benefits that operate independently of psoriasis — the risk-benefit is strongly positive.

How to start
1
Add before you restrict
Rather than cutting foods out, focus on adding vegetables, legumes, and fish first. This avoids the restriction mindset and is more sustainable.
2
Switch your oil
Replacing vegetable and sunflower oil with extra virgin olive oil is one of the highest-impact, lowest-effort changes. Polyphenols in EVOO have direct anti-inflammatory action.
3
Aim for 2–3 fish meals per week
Oily fish is the most concentrated dietary source of EPA and DHA — the omega-3s with the most direct anti-inflammatory evidence. Tinned sardines, mackerel, or salmon count.
Why Good, not Strong?

The 2025 MEDIPSO RCT upgraded this from Moderate to Good — but the trial enrolled 19 people. That's one well-designed study, not a body of replicated large trials. Strong evidence requires multiple independent RCTs with consistent results. We're honest about that distinction. The direction of the evidence is clear; the effect size in a larger replication may differ. What's not in doubt: the diet reduces inflammatory markers and has proven cardiovascular benefit — this will likely help your overall health regardless of what it does to your skin specifically.
