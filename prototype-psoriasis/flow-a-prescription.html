Medical & prescription

6 options — the most effective treatments available. All require a dermatologist. Use these screens to understand what they are and how to ask for them.

ACCESS: Rx — dermatology Clinic — outpatient

All treatments on this page require a dermatologist referral. In England, biologics require a PASI score ≥10 and DLQI score ≥10 (moderate-to-severe impact on quality of life). If you think you qualify, this page will help you have a more informed conversation at your next appointment.

Phototherapy (NB-UVB)
Strong evidence Clinic
Narrowband UVB phototherapy achieves 70% PASI75 in a 2025 meta-analysis of 1,322 patients — among the highest response rates of any non-biologic treatment. Delivered in dermatology outpatient sessions, typically 2–3× per week. The 2024 LITE randomised trial showed home phototherapy devices perform as well as clinic-based treatment. If you're not currently receiving this, ask your dermatologist — it's often skipped over in favour of immediate prescribing.
Methotrexate (oral systemic)
Strong evidence Rx
The most widely used oral treatment for moderate-to-severe psoriasis worldwide. Taken once weekly as a tablet, alongside folic acid to reduce side effects. Requires regular liver function and blood monitoring. NICE first-line systemic treatment before biologics. A 2023 network meta-analysis confirms efficacy. Weight loss significantly improves methotrexate response — if you're on it and overweight, losing even 5–10% of body weight is worth discussing with your dermatologist.
Apremilast (Otezla) — oral PDE4 inhibitor
Good evidence Rx
An oral daily tablet — no injections, no blood monitoring. Positioned between systemic treatment and biologics. PASI75 in around 33% of participants at 16 weeks (lower than biologics, but a valid step for moderate disease). ESTEEM 1 (n=844) and ESTEEM 2 (n=413) registration trials confirm efficacy. Often the right choice for people who want to avoid injections or haven't tolerated methotrexate. NICE-approved.
IL-17 inhibitors (secukinumab / ixekizumab)
Strong evidence Rx
Among the most effective psoriasis treatments currently available. 70–74% of patients achieve PASI90 (90% skin clearance). Secukinumab (Cosentyx) and ixekizumab (Taltz) are both NICE-approved in England. Monthly injections after a loading phase. If you have moderate-to-severe psoriasis and haven't been offered these, ask directly — eligibility requires PASI ≥10 and DLQI ≥10.
IL-23 inhibitors (risankizumab / guselkumab)
Strong evidence Rx
The newest and potentially most durable biologics for psoriasis. Risankizumab (Skyrizi) has the best long-term remission data — drug survival analyses show the highest rates of staying on treatment at 3 and 5 years. PASI90 in ~73% at 16 weeks. Injections every 12 weeks after loading. If you've been on an older biologic for years with partial response, this generation is worth discussing. NICE-approved.
Cyclosporine (short-term systemic)
Strong evidence Rx
A fast-acting oral treatment — often used to gain rapid control during a severe flare, particularly when someone needs clear skin quickly (e.g. a significant life event). Not recommended for long-term use due to kidney and blood pressure effects with time. Requires monitoring. Useful as a bridge while longer-term treatments are established. NICE-approved for moderate-to-severe psoriasis.

Reading the evidence on these treatments gives you better language for your dermatology appointments. You don't need to advocate for specific products — but knowing what the options are and why they differ helps you ask the right questions.
