Rotterdam criteria — your picture
Rotterdam criteria is the clinical checklist doctors use to diagnose PCOS. It has 3 components — a diagnosis only requires 2 out of 3 to be present. Not all three.

📋 Preparing for your GP appointment
▾
How much data to bring: Ideally, track at least 3 full menstrual cycles before your GP appointment — this gives them meaningful cycle length data. If that feels like a long wait, even 6–8 weeks of symptom and cycle logging is a useful starting point. Start tracking now.
What to track
  • Cycle dates — when each period starts and ends, how heavy
  • Symptoms day by day — acne, hair changes, energy, mood, bloating
  • Sleep patterns and stress levels (cortisol affects PCOS significantly)
What to tell your GP
  • Your cycle pattern — how irregular, how long, how heavy
  • Any androgen symptoms (acne, excess hair growth, hair thinning)
  • Family history of PCOS or type 2 diabetes
  • Any weight changes or persistent fatigue
Questions to ask
  • "Can you check my androgens and LH/FSH ratio?"
  • "Should I have a pelvic ultrasound to check for polycystic ovaries?"
  • "Is there anything I can start now while I wait for test results?"
  • "Is my insulin resistance worth investigating — should I check fasting glucose?"
