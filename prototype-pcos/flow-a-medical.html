Medical treatments

Prescription and GP-advised options — all require clinical oversight.

All treatments on this page require a GP or specialist referral. We'll help you prepare for that conversation.
Strong evidence GP required
Metformin
The most commonly prescribed medication for PCOS with insulin resistance. Reduces fasting insulin and androgens, improves menstrual regularity. Well-tolerated with decades of safety data. A frontline option for most presentations.
Good evidence GP required
Combined oral contraceptive pill
Regulates cycle and reduces androgen-driven symptoms (acne, hirsutism). Widely used first-line for PCOS but suppresses natural cycle — not appropriate if trying to conceive. Discuss progesterone-only alternatives with your GP.
Good evidence GP required
Spironolactone
An anti-androgen used off-label for PCOS-related hirsutism and acne. RCTs show significant reductions in hair growth and skin symptoms. Requires contraception during use. Particularly useful when androgen symptoms are prominent.
FERTILITY OPTIONS — SPECIALIST REQUIRED
Strong evidence Specialist
Letrozole (ovulation induction)
Now recommended over Clomifene as first-line ovulation induction for PCOS in most guidelines. RCTs show higher live birth rates. Prescribed by fertility specialists for women with anovulatory infertility.
Good evidence Specialist
Clomifene citrate
The traditional first-line ovulation induction agent — now largely superseded by letrozole for PCOS but still used. Multi-follicular stimulation risk is higher. Requires monitoring. Second-line in current NICE guidelines.
