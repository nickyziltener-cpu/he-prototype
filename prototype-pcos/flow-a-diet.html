Diet & nutrition

Dietary changes that improve insulin sensitivity and reduce PCOS-driven hormone imbalance.

Strong evidence
Low-GI diet
Lower glycaemic foods reduce insulin spikes — the metabolic driver of PCOS symptoms for most people. RCTs consistently show improvements in fasting insulin, testosterone, and menstrual frequency.
Learn more
Good evidence
Anti-inflammatory diet
Chronic low-grade inflammation is a feature of PCOS. A diet rich in vegetables, oily fish, nuts, and olive oil reduces inflammatory markers and may improve hormonal balance.
Good evidence
Mediterranean diet
Broadly consistent with both low-GI and anti-inflammatory principles. Observational and interventional data support improvements in metabolic markers and menstrual regularity.
Moderate evidence
Low-carbohydrate diet
More restrictive than low-GI. Some RCTs show rapid improvements in insulin and androgen markers. Adherence is difficult long-term; evidence for sustained benefit is mixed.
