Strong evidence Diet
Low-GI diet
Choosing lower-glycaemic foods reduces insulin spikes — the core metabolic driver of PCOS symptoms for most people.
Approach
GI < 55
Target
Insulin ↓ · T ↓
Time to effect
4–8 weeks
What it is

The glycaemic index (GI) measures how quickly a food raises blood sugar. Lower-GI foods cause a slower, smaller insulin response. For PCOS, this matters because most PCOS symptoms — irregular cycles, androgen excess, acne — are downstream effects of chronic insulin elevation driving the ovaries to overproduce androgens.

A low-GI diet doesn't restrict calories or eliminate food groups. It's about swap logic: white bread → sourdough or oats, white rice → basmati or legumes, sugary snacks → nuts or fruit with a lower glycaemic response.

How it's used
1
Start with the biggest insulin drivers
Cut sugary drinks, white bread, and ultra-processed snacks first. These create the largest insulin spikes and the easiest wins.
2
Pair carbs with protein or fat
Adding protein or fat to any carbohydrate meal slows glucose absorption and blunts the insulin spike — even for higher-GI foods.
3
Track your cycle as you change your diet
Menstrual changes are your primary indicator. Keep a log — subtle changes in cycle length often appear before you notice anything else.
Foods to emphasise and reduce
Emphasise
  • Oats, barley, rye
  • Legumes & pulses
  • Most vegetables
  • Berries & apples
  • Nuts & seeds
  • Protein with each meal
Reduce
  • White bread & rice
  • Sugary drinks & juice
  • Processed snacks
  • Cornflakes / puffed rice
  • Alcohol
  • Watermelon, melon
What the research shows
RCT · 2020 · n = 85 · 12 weeks
A low-GI diet significantly reduced fasting insulin (−23%), testosterone (−18%), and improved menstrual frequency — all compared to standard healthy eating advice. The effect was independent of caloric intake.
Systematic review · 2021 · 15 studies
Consistently shows improvements in insulin resistance markers regardless of whether weight loss occurs. Effect is independent of caloric restriction — food composition, not just quantity, matters.
What to expect week by week
Weeks 1–2Energy may fluctuate as blood sugar stabilises. Entirely normal — it settles.
Weeks 3–6Fasting insulin typically starts improving. Reduced bloating common in this window.
Weeks 6–12Menstrual changes most likely here. Track cycle start dates carefully.
Long termThis is a sustainable eating pattern, not a short-term diet. The longer it continues, the greater the benefit.
