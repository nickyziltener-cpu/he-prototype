Pelvic floor physiotherapy

Specialist physio targeting pelvic muscle tension — a key pain amplifier in endometriosis. A 2024 systematic review found significant reductions in pelvic pain and pain during sex, with effect sizes comparable to hormonal treatments.

What it is

Chronic pelvic pain — whatever its origin — causes the body to protect itself by tensing pelvic floor muscles. Over time this creates a hypertonic (overactive) pelvic floor that generates pain independently of the underlying endometriosis. This is why some women continue to experience pain even after successful surgical treatment.

A pelvic health physiotherapist assesses and treats this muscle tension through a combination of manual therapy, trigger point release, and guided exercises — both internal and external approaches, always led by patient comfort.

How research suggests using it
1
Assessment (1–2 sessions)
A specialist pelvic health physiotherapist maps your muscle tension patterns, pain distribution, and movement restrictions. They will ask about your history and what triggers symptoms.
2
Treatment course (6–12 sessions)
Typically 6–12 sessions over 2–4 months. Combines hands-on manual therapy with personalised home exercises. Progress is tracked session by session.
3
Self-management ongoing
Home exercise programme to maintain progress. Many women continue monthly or quarterly check-ins to stay on top of flares.
Where this evidence comes from
Strong evidence
A 2024 systematic review pooled evidence from multiple trials of pelvic PT in endometriosis and chronic pelvic pain, finding consistent, significant reductions in pelvic pain, pain during sex and quality-of-life scores across all included studies. Effect sizes were comparable to hormonal treatments.
What this means — a systematic review is higher quality than a single trial because it combines and re-evaluates all available evidence. Consistent effects across multiple studies, multiple countries, and multiple pain types makes this one of the better-supported non-hormonal options for endo pain.

Worth knowing

Pelvic physio is almost always self-pay in the UK (typically £70–120 per session). Some gynaecology teams refer as part of a specialist endo team — it's worth asking. The BSGE (British Society for Gynaecological Endoscopy) lists accredited endo centres where this is more likely to be available via referral. Allow 3–4 months to see meaningful results.
