Diet

4 options, ranked by strength of evidence for endometriosis.

Anti-inflammatory diet
Moderate evidence
Reducing red meat, processed foods, refined sugar and trans fats while increasing omega-3 rich foods, leafy greens and berries. Observational studies consistently link high red-meat intake to increased endometriosis risk and severity — but no RCTs of this dietary pattern in endo exist yet. The association is strong; the causal trial evidence is not.

Omega-3 rich diet
Moderate evidence
Prioritising fatty fish (salmon, mackerel, sardines), flaxseed, walnuts and chia seeds. Omega-3s reduce the inflammation that drives pelvic pain — the same effect as ibuprofen, but through food rather than a pill. The underlying mechanism is well-established; direct trial evidence for the dietary pattern in endometriosis remains limited.
Gluten reduction trial
Moderate evidence
A 2012 Italian study (75 women, no comparison group) found most women with endo reported pain reduction after 12 months gluten-free — despite not having coeliac disease. This is one study, not a controlled trial. The mechanism is unclear and hasn't been replicated at scale. Worth trying if other approaches haven't worked, but go in with realistic expectations.
Low-oestrogen dietary approach
Limited evidence
Reducing foods associated with oestrogen elevation — alcohol, processed soy, red meat and high-fat dairy — on the theory that endometriosis is an oestrogen-driven condition. Plausible mechanistically; direct evidence in humans is limited.
