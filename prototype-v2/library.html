Psychological 4 approaches
You flagged stress as a trigger — here's why it matters

Research shows the gut and brain communicate through a direct neural highway — the vagus nerve. For many people with IBS, stress doesn't just make symptoms feel worse: it physiologically changes gut motility and sensitivity. This is why psychological interventions like gut-directed hypnotherapy and CBT have strong evidence for IBS — they work on the mechanism, not just the symptoms.
